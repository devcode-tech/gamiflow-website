🧪Real-world implementation

How a 22-location taco chain runs a location-vs-location competition.

🌮 Casa Verde Tacos, 22-location fast-casual chain

Casa Verde wants two things at once: more SMS subscribers ahead of a menu launch, and a reason for each location's staff to care about hitting a number, the way a leaderboard makes a sales floor competitive.

The setup

  1. Casa Verde generates a location-specific QR code for each of its 22 stores from Gamiflow. What that code gets printed on is entirely their call, they choose table tents and a small register sticker. Gamiflow doesn't supply or touch any of that hardware.
  2. Entrants submit an email and phone number (SMS opt-in), an optional photo of their meal, and their location, the location is pre-filled from the QR code itself, not self-reported.
  3. Gamification adds bonus entries for following on Instagram or referring a friend who also enters.
  4. A live leaderboard shows entries by location, refreshed daily, so the Round Rock, TX location can see it's ahead of Cedar Park by 40 entries with three days left.
  5. The winning location gets a team lunch funded by corporate and a feature on Casa Verde's Instagram, an incentive staff at that location actually feel.

What Casa Verde walks away with

A larger email and SMS list, segmented by home location from day one, plus real signal on which locations under-index on customer engagement relative to foot traffic, useful for deciding where to focus staff training next.

🛋️Also works for high-ticket retail

When the sale doesn't close today, the follow-up is the whole game.

🛋️ Harlow & Rowe, furniture and mattress showroom, 6 locations

Most furniture shoppers don't buy on their first visit. Harlow & Rowe's sales associates need a fast way to capture a browsing customer's details without an awkward handwritten form, and a clear record of which associate actually spent time with them.

The setup

  1. Each showroom has its own QR code, and each sales associate has their own personal code as well, printed on a small badge or business card they hand over during the conversation.
  2. A customer scans the associate's code, not the generic store code, so the lead is attributed to the person who actually did the work, not just the building.
  3. The form takes email and phone in under fifteen seconds, plus an optional note field the associate can fill in later ("interested in the Ashford sectional, wants it in charcoal").
  4. The captured lead lands in Harlow & Rowe's own CRM, tagged with store, associate, and product interest. Gamiflow does not send any follow-up emails or texts itself. The associate, or the store's own marketing tool, does that follow-up.

Why the employee-level detail matters here

On a high-ticket floor, commission and follow-up ownership both depend on knowing exactly which associate earns credit for a lead. A store-level QR code alone can't answer that. A personal one can.

🔌How it connects to your existing stack

We hand you a code and the data behind it. Everything else stays yours.

01Gamiflow generates a unique QR code and link per store, and per employee if you want it. Printing it on a poster, a register sticker, or a receipt template you already have is on you, we don't sell or set up any hardware.
02Email addresses and phone numbers sync into your own ESP and SMS platform (Klaviyo, Attentive, Podium, or whatever you already run), pre-tagged by store and, where used, by employee.
03Gamiflow doesn't send marketing emails or texts. We capture and attribute the lead, then it's your team, or your store staff directly for high-ticket follow-up, who does the outreach.
04Loyalty-eligible entries can push into your existing loyalty program as a new member record if one already exists.
05Store managers see a permissioned leaderboard, their own store's and employees' numbers, not the full chain-wide customer database.
