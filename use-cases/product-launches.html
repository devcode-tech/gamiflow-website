🧪Real-world implementation

How a DTC headphone brand built 14,000 waitlist signups before launch.

🎧 Nova Audio, DTC headphone brand, new model launch

Nova Audio has a new headphone model six weeks out and no existing list for it. Instead of a plain "notify me" form, they build a referral-powered waitlist where your position depends on who you bring with you.

The setup

  1. Signing up with an email puts you on the waitlist at your current position. Referring a friend who also signs up moves you up the queue, a visible, gamified incentive to share.
  2. A short preference survey asks about color and use case (commute, gym, studio), data Nova's product team actually uses to plan initial inventory splits.
  3. A handful of early-access unboxing units go to influencers in week one, seeding the initial wave of referral traffic into the waitlist.
  4. On launch day, the top 500 referrers get a same-day priority access code, everyone else gets access and a smaller discount code on a staggered schedule over the following week.
  5. SMS opt-in at signup means launch-day notifications land as texts, not emails that get buried.

Why it beat a plain waitlist

A static "notify me" form has no reason to be shared. A queue people can move up does, and that's the entire difference between a list that grows on its own and one that only grows from paid traffic.

🔌How it connects to your existing stack

The waitlist feeds the exact automation launch day depends on.

01Waitlist signups sync to Klaviyo in real time, tagged with referral count so segments like "top 500 referrers" can be built before launch day, not scrambled together at the last minute.
02A pre-built launch day flow in the ESP fires based on queue tier, priority access code to the top segment, staggered codes to everyone else.
03Color and use-case survey data exports to the product team ahead of the initial inventory buy, well before a single unit ships.
